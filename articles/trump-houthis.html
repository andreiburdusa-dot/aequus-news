Trump Refuses Saudi Pressure for Military Action Against Houthis

Published: September 11, 2026

Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman urged President Donald Trump to authorize military intervention against Houthi rebels, requesting U.S. action during a two-hour phone call. Trump refused, citing concerns over escalation within Yemen's ongoing conflict, according to sources cited by HotNews.ro.

The refusal follows rapid Houthi advances along Yemen's Red Sea coast. On September 11, Iran-backed forces completed control of the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, capturing Mayyoun Island (Perim) at its narrowest point. The strategic chokepoint now places Houthi forces directly inside one of the world's most critical maritime routes linking Asia to Europe via the Suez Canal.

Concurrent with territorial gains, Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure have intensified. Mediafax reports 73 documented incidents targeting Saudi Arabia within the recent period, including strikes on civilian infrastructure. Drone strikes originating from Iraq additionally disabled Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline on September 11, forcing temporary shutdown of critical oil export infrastructure.

The situation represents direct blowback from the 2018 U.S. withdrawal from the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA). Iran's subsequent regional arming of proxy forces — including Houthis in Yemen and Hezbollah in Lebanon — has accelerated parallel takeovers of two critical global chokepoints: the Strait of Hormuz (Iran-controlled) and now the Bab al-Mandeb (Houthi-controlled).

Sources: HotNews.ro, Mediafax.ro, Digi24.ro (AFP reporting), U.S. Central Command
